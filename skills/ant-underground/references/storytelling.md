# The story method — how this reel recruits the viewer

This is the part that is not drawing. A reel in this style can be beautiful and still fail, because
the pictures never carry the meaning: **the captions carry the whole story**, and the pictures only
have to make the words land.

## The voice: the character talks to *you*

The reference reel is narrated by the ant, in the second person, in the present tense:

> "Este formigueiro. Formigas. Quero ser. Vira formiga. Quem segue, do céu. Do céu, ela cavou."

Read that as a sentence and the grammar is the key:

- **"Quero ser"** — *I want to be*. First person. The speaker wants something.
- **"Vira formiga"** — *becomes an ant*. The subject of that verb is **the viewer**, not the
  speaker. It has dropped its subject, and the subject is *you*.
- **"Quem segue, do céu"** — *whoever follows, from the sky*. "Segue" = follows. On Instagram
  that is a literal button. "Do céu" = from the sky = the viewer arrives from above.
- **"Do céu, ela cavou"** — *from the sky, she dug*. The ant's passive voice again: things happen
  to the ant. And a second ant — a woman — digs.

So the reel is **not** a story about an ant. It is an ant explaining, in the viewer's language,
what the viewer just did: you followed, so you became one of us. The ant is the mascot; the
viewer is the protagonist.

This is why the style works so well for engagement: **the story is about the person watching.**

## The four-part structure

Every reel in this family has the same four movements. They are not optional; they are the hook.

| Part | What it does | Length | The reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The claim** | Stakes the world and says what this place is | 2–4 s | `ESTE FORMIGUEIRO / FORMIGAS` — the title card, with the golden emblem: this is a place, and there are things in it |
| **2. The want** | The speaker states an ambition the viewer can want too | 3–5 s | `QUER SER / VIRA FORMIGA` — the ant under the lamp, alone, wanting to become |
| **3. The invitation** | The viewer is pulled in — follow, comment, save | 3–5 s | `QUEM SEGUE, DO CÉU` — the lamp, the viewer arriving from above |
| **4. The cliffhanger** | Something unresolved, pointing at the next part | 3–5 s | `DO CÉU, ELA CAVOU` — another dig, deeper down |

The cliffhanger is not decoration. It is the *retention* mechanism: the reel promises a second
part. A reel that resolves completely has nothing to bring the viewer back for.

### The order matters

Claim → want → invitation → cliffhanger. Do not put the invitation first; it reads as a
transaction and people scroll. Do not put the cliffhanger last-but-one; the CTA has to be the
last thing the viewer reads, or they act before they know what they are agreeing to.

The reference puts `QUEM SEGUE` (the invitation) in the *second-to-last* beat and ends on
`DO CÉU, ELA CAVOU` — the tease. So the actual order on screen is:

1. claim · 2. want · 3. the invocation is split across the last two beats · 4. tease

The invitation and the cliffhanger are the same two shots, and the tease is last.

## The rules of the captions

### Short lines, one idea each

Every caption line is at most **two words**. `QUER SER`. `VIRA FORMIGA`. `QUEM SEGUE`. `DO CÉU`.
`ELA CAVOU`. This is not a stylistic flourish — a reel plays muted more often than not, and a
caption is read at a glance in a feed. Two words is a glance.

### The subject is dropped on purpose

`VIRA FORMIGA` has no subject. The viewer supplies it: *you* become an ant. `QUEM SEGUE` names
the condition explicitly (*whoever follows*). The reference then repeats the dropped subject in
the next shot's caption (`DO CÉU, ELA CAVOU` — *she* dug), which is what makes the pattern
readable.

### The grammar of the video, not the grammar of the prose

The captions are not sentences. They are a **score**: subject, verb, and a couple of nouns. When
you write the storyboard, write the captions as fragments first and only then decide what the
pictures have to show.

### Two lines per shot, always

Line 1 sets it up. Line 2 lands it, about 1.2 s later. The reference never uses one line alone
except in the tease, where the second line does the work of a full stop.

### The last word of the reel is a question the viewer has to leave to answer

The cliffhanger must be a **concrete image of something unfinished** — a dig that went deeper, a
door that opened, a light that went out — not an abstract tease. The viewer has to be able to
picture the next part, or they will not come back for it.

## The engagement mechanics, and where they hide

The reference never says "follow me" or "like this video". It **makes the action the premise**:
following is how you get down here, and getting down here is how you become an ant. The CTA is
inside the fiction.

That is the trick worth copying:

| The plain version | The version in this style |
|---|---|
| "Follow for part 2!" | "Quem segue, do céu" — *whoever follows, from the sky* |
| "Like and comment!" | "Quem comenta, trabalha" — *whoever comments, works* |
| "Subscribe!" | the story never resolves |

The viewer performs the CTA without being asked to, because the CTA **is** the story. If you
write "SEGUIR" in the caption, you have thrown away the entire mechanic and replaced it with an
advertisement.

### Reward the comment

The reference's own line is "quem comenta, que trabalho terá" — *whoever comments, what work will
they have*. So the comment is framed as **being given a job in the colony**, not as a like. The
viewer's comment is a role in the world. That is worth copying for any comment-bait reel: the
reward must be a *part in the story*, not a prize.

## The visual grammar supports the words

The pictures are not decorative, but they are not literal either. They have jobs:

| Caption | Picture | Job |
|---|---|---|
| `ESTE FORMIGUEIRO / FORMIGAS` | the title card, surface over underground, the golden emblem between the lines | the claim needs a *place* — so the frame is split between above and below, and the emblem is the seal of the place |
| `QUER SER / VIRA FORMIGA` | the lamp, the ant alone under it, in the pool of light | the want is *solitude under a light you cannot reach* |
| `QUEM SEGUE, DO CÉU` | the lamp bigger, the ant under it, mushrooms flanking | the invitation is *arriving from above* — so the light is overhead |
| `DO CÉU, ELA CAVOU` | the surface, grass, the ant digging with a sickle | the tease is *another dig, in daylight* — so we cut to the sky |

Three rules:

1. **The light is the invitation.** The lamp is the only warm thing in a dark world, and it is
   always overhead. "Come from the sky" and "there is light above" are the same image.
2. **Cut to the sky for the outside world.** Every time the story leaves the colony, the frame
   goes to the surface: orange sky, green stalks, hard horizontal ground line. The underground
   is the inside; the sky is the invitation.
3. **The emblem is the seal.** The golden ring on the title card says *this is a place with a
   name*. Use it on the claim and on the closer, and nowhere else.

## Writing one

Work in this order. It is the same order the reel is watched in.

1. **Write the claim.** What is this place, and what is in it? One noun, one noun.
2. **Write the want.** What does the speaker want, in the first person? `QUER SER` + a noun.
3. **Write the invitation.** What did the viewer just do, in the second person? Name the
   condition: *quem segue*, *quem comenta*, *quem salva*.
4. **Write the cliffhanger.** One concrete unfinished image, with a subject that is not the
   speaker.
5. **Now check the grammar.** Each caption should be a fragment, ≤ 2 words per line, and the
   dropped subjects should line up across shots.
6. **Now decide the pictures.** For each caption, one image, and one job for that image.
7. **Now decide the CTA.** It must be inside the fiction. If you cannot phrase it as a fact about
   the world, do not include it — a reel with no CTA is fine, a reel with an ad in it is not.

## What not to do

- **Do not write sentences.** `QUEM SEGUE, DO CÉU, VIRA FORMICA` is not a caption; `QUEM SEGUE` is.
- **Do not put the CTA in words.** The reference never writes "follow".
- **Do not resolve.** The reel must end on something unfinished.
- **Do not reuse the world's nouns for the CTA.** "Formiga" is the mascot; if the caption says
  "follow to become an ant", "follow" has to mean the button, not a literal ant following.
- **Do not make the cliffhanger abstract.** "Algo mais abaixo" is weak. "Ela cavou" with the
  camera on a fresh hole is strong, because the viewer can picture it.
