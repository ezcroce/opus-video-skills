# Sound: mixing, levels, mastering

The chain in `lib/build.mjs`, in order. Every number is a config key in `podcast.config.json`, so you can change the
sound without touching the code.

## 1. Voice

Each clip is high-passed at 80 Hz (two one-pole stages, 12 dB/oct) to remove rumble, desk thumps and DC offset —
cheap TTS and laptop recordings all carry some. Then summed to mono and placed at its timeline start with
`voiceGain` (default 0.92). No per-clip normalisation: two speakers recorded at different levels should be balanced by
choosing the right voice, not by flattening both.

What to listen for: sibilance (a hard "s" that cuts through the bed), plosives on short lines (a line starting with a
hard consonant lands on the beat and pops), and level jumps between two voices. Level jumps are fixed in `voices.json`
(recast or re-synthesise), not with a fader.

## 2. The bed

`assets/music/bed.wav` — a loopable music bed, generated by `lib/synth.mjs` at scaffold time (four bars, ~100 BPM, a
slow chord cycle, a felt-piano pulse on the beat). It is crossfaded (1.4 s, equal power) to the full episode length and
played at `music.gain` (default 0.16 → about −16 dB under the voice).

- **Ducking** is done by the music's own level, not by a sidechain compressor. At 0.16 the consonants stay clear and the
  bed is felt rather than heard. If you can name the chord progression while someone is talking, it is 3–5 dB too loud.
- **Fades.** 1 s in, 2 s out. The bed never starts or stops with the voice.
- **Replacing it.** Any loop works; keep it seamless (its own start and end must match) or the crossfade will click. A
  loop with a strong melody fights speech — prefer pads, pulses, room tone, or a single sustained note.
- **Turning it off.** `"music": { "enabled": false }`. The sting and tag still play; the episode becomes dry, which is
  often the right choice for a serious interview.

## 3. Sting, chapters, tag

- **Sting** (`assets/stingers/sting.wav`, 5 s): a rising arpeggio over a pad, placed at the top. `beats.open` (2.4 s)
  is the minimum air after it, so the first line lands after the sting has decayed, not on top of it.
- **Chapter breaks** are silence, not music. They are the listener's map; a musical change under them implies a section
  change that the script may not mean. `beats.chapter` 1.6 s, `paragraphGap` 0.55 s, `gap` 0.22 s (between turns).
- **Tag** (`assets/stingers/tag.wav`, 4 s): three falling notes with a long tail, after `beats.close` (3.2 s) of air.
  It is the "that's the episode" sound; keep it identical across a series.

Regenerate any of them deterministically:

```bash
node lib/synth.mjs --kind=sting --out=assets/stingers/sting.wav
node lib/synth.mjs --kind=tag   --out=assets/stingers/tag.wav
node lib/synth.mjs --kind=bed --bpm=92 --out=assets/music/bed.wav
```

## 4. Master

- Peak-normalise the whole mix to `truePeakDb` (default −1.5 dBFS) — one gain for the episode, so the internal balance
  is preserved. Target −1.5, not −0.1: lossy encoders overshoot on transients.
- Soft-knee limit above −6 dBFS (`ceiling` 0.94) with a tanh curve: transparent on speech, and it stops a rare clipped
  clip from poking through.
- Output: 16-bit PCM WAV at `rate` (44.1 kHz). The WAV is the deliverable; `export.mjs` makes the MP3 (192 kbps,
  mono-safe, chapter markers in ID3v2.3).

Speech-first loudness lands around −16 LUFS with this chain, which is right for podcast platforms. If the platform
normalises to −14 LUFS (most do), the episode will be turned *up* slightly — that is safe, whereas mastering loud and
being turned down loses the transients. Do not chase LUFS number by number without measuring; the chain here is
deliberately conservative.

## 5. Review, by ear and by eye

`out/check/waveform.png` — 3 px per second, one red tick per line start, chapter marks in accent red. Look for:

- **Flat gaps** where a line should be: a missing clip, or one that came out silent.
- **Solid blocks** at the top and bottom of the band: the limiter is working too hard, meaning the mix was too hot
  before mastering (lower `voiceGain`, not `truePeakDb`).
- **Chapter marks closer than ~2 min**: the episode is over-chaptered, fold two chapters together.
- **A visibly uneven start**: the first line too close to the sting — raise `beats.open`.

Then listen in `out/player.html`: the transcript scrolls with the audio, so a line that reads fine but lands badly is
easy to find and fix in the script.
